# Marlin — Script v2 (draft for client review)

Rewritten from the client's "simple script" notes. **One mission, told in three beats people can repeat back:**

> **1. Get there. 2. See it. 3. Stay on it.**

The audience should leave with those three words and the tagline, *Speed is security.*
Suspects are never named or described: on screen and in VO it's just a "vessel", "target" or "contact".

- **VO:** ~100 words, about 35s of speech at the scratch voice's pace. That leaves room for music, engine and silence. (v1 was 137 words and didn't fit 60s.)
- **Source material:** the client notes, Storyboard V3, and archimedesfreight.com ("Building the Future Standard in Shipping", "It all starts with defense", "Reducing a 1,000 nm mission from 29 to 10 hours").

---

## ACT 1 · The gap (0:00–0:12)

**1 · Night, open water (0:00–0:06)**
Dark sea, coastline lights far off. Cut to the port-ops console: one orange contact, `NO AIS`, running fast toward the coast.
VO: *"Every night, small, fast boats run dark toward our coast."*
SFX: wind, distant outboard whine, console beep.

**2 · Conventional patrol (0:06–0:12)**
A generic, unmarked patrol boat still at the pier. Clock arc sweeps. The orange track on the console keeps moving.
VO: *"Conventional patrols take time to launch, and rough water slows them down. By the time they arrive, the target is gone."*
SFX: ticking builds → hard cut to silence.

## ACT 2 · Marlin: three beats (0:12–0:47)

**3 · Marlin comes to life (0:12–0:17)**
Silence. At its berth, Marlin's lights come on, the engine spools up, and it pulls away and rises onto its foils. Title lands.
VO: *"Archimedes Freight presents Marlin."*
On screen: ARCHIMEDES logo · MARLIN
SFX: engine spool, score lifts.

**4 · Beat 1: GET THERE (0:17–0:27)**
Tracking shot: Marlin flying level over chop and closing on the contact. Speed readout climbs. Map inset shows the gap shrinking.
VO: *"One: it gets there first. Flying on foils above the chop, at up to 150 knots."*
On screen: `01 · GET THERE` · `UP TO 150 KT*`
Optional proof point from the website: `1,000 NM MISSION · 29 HRS → 10 HRS*`
Footnote: `*Design target`

**5 · Beat 2: SEE IT (0:27–0:36)**
Marlin holds at a standoff. HUD view: a generic sensor sweep passes over the vessel, a box locks on, and the label resolves to `SUSPECT VESSEL · IDENTIFIED`.
VO: *"Two: it sees the threat. Its sensors scan and identify the vessel from a safe distance."*
On screen: `02 · SEE IT`
SFX: soft UI tones, one lock-on chime.

**6 · Beat 3: STAY ON IT (0:36–0:47)**
The vessel turns and runs. Marlin trails it easily, **blue lights flashing**. A teal data link arcs to a generic patrol cutter (team colors, no names or agency markings) that is closing in.
VO: *"Three: it stays on target. Marlin holds the track and streams a live picture to responders, until they make the stop."*
On screen: `03 · STAY ON IT` · `TRACK SHARED → PARTNER`
SFX: hull slap, wind, unintelligible radio chatter.

## ACT 3 · The outcome (0:47–1:00)

**7 · Recap (0:47–0:55)**
The cutter is alongside the vessel; Marlin peels off and holds at a distance. Pull back to the calm coastline at dawn. The three beats appear in turn, on score hits.
VO: *"Get there. See it. Stay on it."*
On screen: `GET THERE · SEE IT · STAY ON IT`

**8 · End card (0:55–1:00)**
Navy. Logo, MARLIN, *Speed is security.*, CEO contact (hold ≥3s), disclaimer.
VO: *"Marlin. Speed is security."*
SFX: final score hit, one engine rev to black.

## Alternate three-beat wordings
| Option | On screen | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **A (recommended)** | GET THERE · SEE IT · STAY ON IT | Plain English, memorable, easy to say in a meeting |
| B | REACH · IDENTIFY · TRACK | More operational/government |
| C | FAST · CLEAR · CONSTANT | Most "brand", least literal |

## What changed from Storyboard V3
- **Two missions → one.** Harbor security is dropped; the film follows a single interdiction, as in the client notes. A shorter story is easier to remember.
- **The four-word beat (Detect · Close · Identify · Hand off) → three beats.**
- **New:** Marlin "comes to life" at the berth, the blue lights, and the live data link to the cutter.
- **Kept:** the tagline, the 150 KT design-target footnote, the console/clock opening, the end card.
- Brief: *"Any material narrative changes require Archimedes' approval"*, so this needs their sign-off.

## Notes on the client's list (to check with Archimedes)
1. **"Sensors detect contraband."** I wrote it as *identifies the vessel*, not *detects contraband*. Claiming the sensors can see cargo is a sensor-performance claim (the storyboard rules these out without data), and it also implies a search. If they *can* claim it, we'd need their approval and wording.
2. **Blue lights flashing.** In the U.S., flashing blue lights signal law enforcement. On Marlin they could suggest Marlin itself has authority to stop boats, which the storyboard rules out ("Marlin detects, tracks and cues"). Options: keep blue with their sign-off, or use a white/amber strobe that reads as "visible and on scene".
3. **"Arch Drone."** Is Marlin uncrewed (drone) or crewed? The storyboard sketch has a cabin. The script works either way, but the visuals (launch, cockpit, lights) depend on the answer.
4. **Coast Guard.** Shown only as a generic patrol cutter: colors and shape, no names, seals or hull markings. The VO says "responders", never an agency.
5. **Website stat (1,000 nm: 29 → 10 hours).** Strong proof of "response time is the gap". Is it a design target, and may we use it?
